## About：完整版

I work at the intersection of clinical research, statistics, and applied AI. I am a Data Analyst at the University of Pittsburgh and a Statistician at the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs through an IPA assignment, working with real-world healthcare data.

My work spans SQL-based data extraction, cohort construction, variable definition, study design, biostatistical modeling, and clinical NLP. I collaborate with clinical teams to translate research questions into reproducible analyses with clear assumptions and well-defined outcomes.

My research examines how measurement, variable definitions, and modeling choices affect what we can conclude from data. I am interested in where bias, measurement error, and uncertainty enter as data become evidence, and in approaches that make those problems visible and assessable. My methodological interests include model validation, causal inference, and network analysis, with applications in healthcare utilization, aging, and dementia.

I hold a Ph.D. in Environmental Engineering from Carnegie Mellon University. My earlier research in environmental sensing and field screening shaped how I think about data: every number has a measurement process behind it, and that process matters for the decisions built on it. This perspective also connects my work to energy systems and sustainability assessment.

I welcome conversations with people working on clinical AI, real-world evidence, and reproducible data science.

Research portfolio: https://hwcmu.github.io/
