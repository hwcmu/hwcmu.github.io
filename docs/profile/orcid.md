## Biography

I am a Data Analyst at the University of Pittsburgh and a Statistician at the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs through an IPA assignment, working on clinical research using real-world healthcare data. I hold a Ph.D. in Environmental Engineering from Carnegie Mellon University.

My research examines how measurement, variable definitions, and modeling choices affect the validity of scientific conclusions and decisions. I am particularly interested in identifying and addressing bias, measurement error, and uncertainty as data are transformed into evidence, including whether a model's prediction target matches its intended use and how errors in model-derived variables affect downstream estimates.

My current work focuses on cohort construction, biostatistical modeling, and clinical AI. My methodological interests include clinical natural language processing, model validation, causal inference, and network analysis, with applications in healthcare utilization, aging, and dementia.

My engineering background extends this perspective to environmental field screening, sustainability assessment, and energy systems. Across these domains, I aim to develop reproducible and interpretable approaches that connect complex data to reliable evidence for research and decision-making.
