A repair enters a structure that already has a history. Reviewing microbial calcium carbonate precipitation for historical architecture restoration brought that boundary into focus: closing a crack is only part of what a repair has to accomplish.

## Repair is a question of compatibility

This work was a mini-review, examining how microbially induced calcium carbonate precipitation might support architectural conservation. The literature raised questions about compatibility, penetration depth, and long-term durability. An incompatible repair can cause further cracking; an apparently successful intervention still has to coexist with the material around it.

## Intervening without breaking the host

The idea I take from this work is simple: a useful intervention has to respect the scale and tolerance of the system it enters.

That boundary also matters when an AI model enters a hospital. Its performance is one question; how it fits into a clinical workflow is another. Alert fatigue, changes in clinician behavior, and effects on care belong in the picture. I connect these fields through that question of fit, rather than treating a repair material and a clinical model as the same kind of intervention.
