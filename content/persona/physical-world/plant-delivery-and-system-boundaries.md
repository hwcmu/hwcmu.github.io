A nanocarrier that delivers its payload while damaging leaf tissue would defeat its own purpose. That was the boundary I drew in my earlier reflection: successful delivery has to make sense within the living system that receives it.

## The system sets the conditions

In this collaborative study, we investigated high-aspect-ratio polymer nanocarriers for DNA delivery and expression in plants. The paper examined how carrier geometry and charge affected delivery within the tested conditions. Those conditions matter: a result in one experimental setting is not a promise that the same design will work in every plant or application.

## What counts as a useful intervention?

This work and my review of historical architecture restoration looked unrelated on the surface. Yet both raised a question about introducing something external into a structure that has its own constraints.

That question remains useful to me in health AI. A model can achieve a strong prediction result while leaving open the harder question of how it will affect the workflow around it. The connection is a personal way of thinking about interventions: pay attention to the receiving system, not only to the thing being introduced.
