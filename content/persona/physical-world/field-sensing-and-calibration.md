In a benchmark dataset, numbers arrive neatly aligned in a CSV. Outdoors, obtaining a useful reading depends on the soil, the sampling method, and whether a sensor's calibration holds beyond the conditions in which it was developed.

## A sensor has to work in a setting

Our study compared thermal-desorption gas chromatography and portable near-infrared sensing for screening petroleum hydrocarbons in soil. The comparison considered analytical performance alongside the practical demands of robotic deployment. A prototype incorporating the NIR sensor was tested in controlled outdoor conditions; broader field deployment remained a separate question.

## What I carry into data work

You stop reading a column as ground truth and start reading it as the output of a fallible instrument operating under real-world friction. That is the part of this work that stays with me: a reading carries the conditions under which it was obtained.

Healthcare records have their own version of that friction—billing codes lag behind clinical reality, lab orders reflect local hospital habits, and timestamps slip. The connection I draw is methodological: before asking what a model can learn from a column, ask how that column came to exist.
