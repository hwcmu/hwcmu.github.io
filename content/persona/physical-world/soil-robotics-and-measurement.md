Later in my PhD, I collaborated with robotics and environmental teams on autonomous soil sampling. Watching hardware struggle to capture a single reliable reading changes how you look at a table of data: the table begins long before anyone opens it.

## Measurement is part of the system

The paper considers sensing, sampling, mobility, and autonomy together, including a prototype for characterizing salt-impacted soil. These components determine what can be measured and under which conditions. My credited contributions to this paper were visualization and writing review and editing; the robotic system was a collaborative effort.

## A dataset has a physical beginning

What stays with me is the distance between a tidy column and the process needed to produce it. Getting to the sampling location, accessing the soil, and obtaining a reading are part of the evidence, even when those steps disappear from the final table.

I carry that perspective into healthcare data. Before interpreting a value, I want to understand the process that produced the record. The questions change from sensors and sampling to clinical workflows and documentation, but the habit of looking upstream remains.
